## 5. Strategy and Business Themes

**Anti-drone systems**
- Zen positions its wideband (band-independent) detect-and-jam capability as differentiated. Most Indian anti-drone systems cover only commercial frequencies, and Zen says it has seen no other company with an indigenously designed wideband system.
  > most of the anti-drone systems that have been developed in the country, they are meant to handle commercial frequencies and not non-commercial frequencies.
**Competition and moat**
- Zen was the single vendor in the 2023 tenders. In the last RFP, 12 players attended the pre-bid but only two attended trials, and indigenous-content (IDDM) verification is strict (software code, PCB files, version control). **[CHECK: NUMBER MISMATCH: 2023]**
  > there were 12 people who came for the pre-bid, but only two came to the actual trial.
- IDDM checks include source code review, Gerber files, CAD drawings and version control, which management expects to filter out pretenders.
  > we are going to go through your software code.
- IP is only partly patented. Cutting-edge algorithms are deliberately left unpatented to prevent foreign copying.
  > some parts are patented but some parts are not patented due to the sensitivity in them
- Customisation speed is claimed as a competitive advantage in overseas markets such as NATO and the US.
  > our agility and speed of customizing the product to their needs is much quicker than any of the competition that we have.
**Anti-drone systems**

- Chinese rare earth restrictions have not affected Zen's electronics-based products, and China dependence is described as almost zero.
  > most of our products are electronic-based, and China-dependent is almost zero

  > except our subsidiary Vector Technics, I think they may be having some impact on there because of the magnets that you were talking about

## 7. Analyst's Read

**Positives**
- Consolidated operational EBITDA margin of 40.90% is above the 35% benchmark, and standalone margin at 34.26% is near it despite a 55% revenue drop. This suggests cost discipline and a healthy margin structure.
- Subsidiaries (ARIPL, UTS) are visibly contributing: guided to add about Rs.250 Crores of revenue at about 35% EBITDA and 25% PAT margin. ARI moving to 100% ownership increases the share of profit that accrues to Zen.
- Management says the Rs.60-70 Crores of deferred revenue is already almost executed, so a Q2 catch-up looks likely.
- Zen reports being the sole qualified vendor for the roughly Rs.650 Crores Army simulator order, with no competitor. That would more than double the current Rs.605 Crores standalone book if it converts.
- Capex is modest (Rs.40-50 Crores) and the company avoids pre-buying inventory for orders, which supports cash generation and balance-sheet discipline.
- Optionality in emergency procurement, hard kill, exports (H2 FY26, big FY27) and the US/NATO channel via ARI are credible growth drivers if specs and tenders firm up.

**Concerns**
- Revenue is lumpy and dependent on a few large government orders. Standalone revenue fell 55% and consolidated 38% YoY, and management guides FY26 as muted without any figure.
- Order inflow has been very slow for about a year, and the Rs.650 Crores order has already slipped relative to earlier expectations. Timing risk is high, with the signing date outside Zen's control.
- The current order book of Rs.754 Crores includes Rs.260 Crores of AMC and Rs.148 Crores in subsidiaries, leaving only Rs.346 Crores of Zen equipment orders. This is thin against the 6000 plus Crores three-year ambition.
- Late-stage spec changes by the customer caused the Q1 deferral, which signals execution and customer-change risk in evolving anti-drone programmes, a segment where specs are still being finalised.
- Standalone PAT margin (33.44%) is boosted by other income, since operating margin is 34.26% and total EBITDA is stated at 40-52%. Earnings quality depends partly on treasury income, which was not quantified.
- Other expenses rose to 7.35% of revenue from 5.5% a year ago, as the cost base did not shrink with revenue.
- Exports and the US/NATO opportunity are described only qualitatively, with no order values, and are for FY27, so they cannot support near-term numbers.
- To reach the 6000 plus Crores target, FY27 and FY28 would need very large step-ups, which hinge on orders not yet received.

**Evasive or Unverifiable Claims**
- Management would not give FY26 revenue guidance, even whether it would be in line with FY25, saying it depends on the Q2-end order book.
  > we are not able to put a real figure onto this, whether it will be as much as or little less or a little more.
- Management would not give a Q2 execution figure or quarterly guidance.
  > we would not be able to give the specific guidance on a quarter on that.
- Management would not quantify the anti-drone emergency procurement opportunity.
- The product and Army segment of the Rs.650 Crores simulator order were not disclosed ('little sensitive'). **[CHECK: NUMBER MISMATCH: 650]**
  > I think as the orders come across. They are little sensitive things, Harshad and sometimes the government does not like it.
- Countries for exports were not named, so the export pipeline cannot be independently assessed.
  > we never name the countries.
- Claims such as being top 2-3 globally, no other indigenous wideband player, 7-8% simulation market share and Operation Sindoor testimonials cannot be verified from the transcript.
- Whether the 6000 plus Crores is cumulative over FY26-FY28 was not stated explicitly, and no FY25 base or yearly split was given.
- The simulator revenue series (60, 160, 430, 930 Crores) is cited without naming the metric or fiscal years.
- Other income and treasury income were not quantified despite its effect on PAT.

- Standalone operational EBITDA margin was stated as 34.26% in prepared remarks and 34.27% in Q&A.
- Total standalone EBITDA margin was described as 'more than 40%' in prepared remarks and 'around 52%' in Q&A.
